Frozen Asset-Text Embeddings for Repeat-Preserving
Financial Recommendation
Mikhail Dmitrenko · Team HuggyWuggy
midmitrenko@edu.hse.ru · 2026-11-13
LLM4Rec, HSE University · Session 1: Research Question and Minimal Study Plan · Encoder track
Abstract
Repeat purchases are important in financial recommendation, so replacing a strong history-based ranking can
obscure the value of semantic item representations. This study proposes to retain Latest buy's historical prefix
and replace only the free-slot TF-IDF representation with frozen Qwen3 asset-text embeddings, holding
information and scoring rules fixed. Completed classical experiments on 10,404 warm users give nDCG@10 of
0.0530 for popularity-plus-TF-IDF fill versus 0.0526 for original Latest buy, while the constrained oracle reaches
0.0637. The LLM comparison remains planned: the contribution is a controlled test of incremental
representation value, not a claim of improved investment quality.
1. Introduction
FAR-Trans contains purchases, sales and asset information for financial recommendation [1], [6]. Under the
course project's repeat-allowed task, a model should predict future purchases of either previously bought or
new-to-user assets. This differs from the paper's new-asset-only evaluation. A semantic encoder may improve
discovery, but it should be tested without changing a useful repeat-purchase mechanism.
Research question. We test whether frozen LLM-derived asset-text embeddings improve mean nDCG@10 over
popularity-plus-TF-IDF filling for FAR-Trans users in the course starter's full warm cohort, while keeping Latest
buy's historical prefix unchanged.
Element
Definition for this study
Intervention
Replace TF-IDF item vectors with frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B vectors only.
Control
Popularity-plus-TF-IDF fill using the same pre-cutoff asset names and metadata.
Outcome
Mean nDCG@10 against future eligible purchases over all selected users, including empty truth.
Population
Current teaching cohort: 10,404 warm users; cutoff 2021-06-01; June purchase window.
Track and state
Encoder; partly run. Classical diagnostics are complete; LLM inference and its main comparison are
planned.
Research importance. Repeat/explore modeling [2] and text-based item representations [3] are established,
but frozen content features can underperform learned ID representations [5]. The new knowledge sought is
whether a frozen semantic encoder adds ranking value specifically in free slots after a fixed recency prefix and
a strong popularity term. A negative or null result would bound the usefulness of this low-training-cost design.
Business value. A recommendation product owner must decide whether to retain a cheap classical fill or pay
the preparation, memory and maintenance costs of cached neural embeddings. A same-information
comparison can support that decision even if the LLM does not improve ranking. Neither nDCG nor this offline
study establishes profitability, suitability, causal business effects or user acceptance [1].
Scope. The team has agreed to use the course starter as its common working protocol [8]. Its dates, cohort and
eligibility settings are teaching choices, not a declaration of the final instructor-defined project benchmark. The
output remains one Top-10 list per evaluated user.

Nearest prior work. FLARKO [4] is the closest financial-domain LLM study: it already evaluates KG-grounded,
KTO-finetuned recommendations on FAR-Trans using purchase/profitability hit metrics. Yuan et al. [5] remain
the closest methodological precedent for matched encoder comparisons and frozen versus end-to-end learning.
UniSRec [3] provides modular text features with learned adaptation; RepeatNet [2] motivates repeat/explore
separation.
Gap. FLARKO already studies KTO-finetuned LLM recommendations on FAR-Trans using knowledge-graph
context and 180-day Hits@3 objectives [4]. Among the studies reviewed, we did not find a same-information
comparison of frozen language-model asset-text embeddings against popularity-plus-TF-IDF filling that holds a
recency-based historical prefix fixed and evaluates 30-day, repeat-allowed purchases with all-user mean
nDCG@10. This is a bounded literature finding, not a global novelty claim.
Terminology. An asset new to one user's retained history can still be popular across other users. This is not
the same as an item absent from all training interactions, the stronger cold/new-item condition considered in [5].
Moreover, reconstructed initial positions are excluded by the starter, so “new-to-user” here does not establish
first-ever ownership. The present warm-user cohort is not a warm-item-only benchmark.
Evidence for these summaries. Section and table pointers, qualifications, checked links and complete author
lists are retained in literature/reading_notes.md, literature/link_checks.json and
literature/references.bib. The reviewed representation models should not all be described as prompted
generative LLMs.

assets with at least one positive pre-cutoff close price [8]. The full warm cohort contains 10,404 users and 767

Buy activity and an as-of profile. The label window is [2021-06-01, 2021-07-01). Repeat purchases remain

deterministic ID hashing; this report's main experiments use the full warm cohort.

field-level historical versioning remains a limitation.

3.3. Controlled encoder comparison — planned
Freeze Latest buy's prefix: up to ten historical assets ordered by last Buy date (ties by ISIN). Fill candidates are
all eligible assets outside it. Both encoders receive identical as-of names, category, subcategory, sector,
industry and market, omitting missing fields. Profiles, future transactions and returns are excluded.
Control: TF-IDF name unigrams and field-qualified tokens. Intervention: frozen
Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B [7], revision 97b0c614be4d77ee51c0cef4e5f07c00f9eb65b3; 1024-dimensional
normalized vectors, last-token pooling, no instructions or finetuning. Both use the same normalized
purchase-count-weighted history sum and free-slot score:
s(u,i) = distinct_buyers(i) / max_j distinct_buyers(j) + lambda × cosine(v_u, v_i)
Lambda = 1 for both in the primary swap; no June tuning. Ablations: TF-IDF substitutes the representation
using the same information; lambda = 0 removes similarity and recovers original Latest buy. All supplied
baselines are retained; the diagnostic oracle never supplies inputs.
Metric: binary nDCG@10 uses eligible June purchases. DCG sums rel_r / log2(r+1); IDCG places up to ten
relevant assets first. Empty truth scores zero. Report all-user and nonempty means/counts, plus repeat/new
hits. Ground truth is purchases, not an LLM judge.
Runtime/cost: time cold generation (loading, representation preparation, scoring/Top-10), excluding
evaluation; separate preparation and cached generation. Use 5 repeats on identical hardware, report
median/range, dependencies, precision, batch size and peak memory. Local inference needs no paid API; log
compute hours and actual rental charges. Next sessions: implement/cache embeddings and controls, then
ablate, analyse failures and integrate under the final protocol. Frozen inference avoids sampling; diagnostic
random fills use saved seeds.

5. Discussion
Position and interpretation. I adopt fixed-prefix filling to isolate an Encoder change while protecting repeat
recommendations. The data contain 1,548 repeat and 1,008 new future user–asset pairs (60.6% repeats).
There are 9,553 users with free slots, 726 with future purchases. Pure similarity fill loses to popularity; mixtures
improve slightly. The oracle ceiling is 0.0637, not a promised neural gain. Positive findings would support
cached semantic features for this branch; negative/null findings would support classical fills.
Threats and failure mechanisms. June informed exploratory design and is not a pristine holdout; freeze
settings before the LLM comparison and assess generalization under the final protocol. Empty truth dominates,
full prefixes prevent intervention for some users, and short/multilingual names may provide weak semantics.
The checkpoint postdates prediction time, leaving pretraining-contamination risk. Keep pseudonymized
customer data local and treat text as data, not instructions. Ranking accuracy does not establish suitability or
profit.
Automated metric check, not yet personal verification. One demo user's single relevant asset is at
popularity rank 2 and Latest buy's first fill rank 7. IDCG = 1; rounded scores 1/log2(8) → 0.3333 and 1/log2(3)
→ 0.6309 match per_user.csv. Its excluded initial position means novelty is relative to retained history.
Evidence: outputs/demo_ndcg_nonzero/evidence.json.
